This newsletter has three rules: four minutes, one productivity tip, no feelings. Today we are breaking the third. Tonight at the Monumental, Lionel Messi plays his last match in the Argentina shirt, and the author has been looking at the same cell in the same spreadsheet since 9:14. The agents can wait. That is, technically, their job.

So the edition about Copilot's Researcher, Analyst and Cowork is written, reviewed and parked. You will get it next Tuesday, when you can read again. Today is about the boy from Rosario who was too small, and what you can do with AI to remember him properly: a quiz you will argue about, one fact a day on your phone, and a small app that asks the AI the three questions worth asking tonight, in your language.

Not a football person? There is a small box for you below. It is small on purpose.

The Leo Quiz: casual, fan, or rosarino?

Twelve questions, from the napkin to Lusail. 3 · Ask

Leo, in your language

Three questions worth asking an AI tonight, packed into one small app: type your country and your language once, tap a button, and ChatGPT, Claude or Gemini opens with the question already written.

  • Messi versus your country. Every match he played against your national team, with dates and sources, and how your fans relate to him. This building has 189 member countries and, since this morning, one topic of conversation. We tested it with Peru: 10 matches, 48 seconds, and the night in 2023 when the Lima crowd chanted his name while losing.
  • Relátamelo. A radio commentator from your city narrates your favourite goal in your language. Headphones, please. Think of the people next to you. A Peruvian gets it in Lima slang, a Kenyan in Sheng, and both send it to their father.
  • For the kid who asked why you were crying. A 350-word bedtime story and a colouring page. Celia, the injections, the boy who was too small. Both index fingers, every goal, for Celia, who took him to training and died when he was ten.

For colleagues who do not care about football

This box is small on purpose. Two things in it. First, the quiz is a generic tool: Messi is the sample data, and the same page makes one on your sector for Friday's team meeting. Second, for the economists, the one Messi story that is really about our work: a boy with a treatable condition his city could not finance, and what happened when someone did. Ask any chatbot: "In 400 words, the story of Messi's growth-hormone treatment, the move to Barcelona, and what it says about talent, health and who gets a chance." It is not a football story. It is a human capital story with a happy ending.

Three lines. Where you were, who you were with, what you did at the final whistle. A hospital corridor in Nairobi watching on a phone.
