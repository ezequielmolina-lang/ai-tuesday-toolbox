Five mornings

Somewhere in your files there is a document you are responsible for and have never read all the way through. From this week, you do not have to read it. You take it: three minutes a morning, for five mornings.

3 November 2026 • The AI Tuesday Toolbox

Let us be honest about the document. It is the PAD of the project you lead, or the Operations Manual, or the legal agreement, or the 180-page evaluation of the project that came before yours. You read the summary. The Board read the summary. The lawyer read page 41 and billed for it. Then it went into the folder every team has, the one called Final, and it has been there, unread and binding, ever since.

We asked ourselves what AI is actually good at, and the answer is not "writing another document". It is turning a document you will never read into something you will: one short lesson a day, with a question that makes you guess first and a page reference you can check. The format comes from the courses we have all been meaning to take. The content comes from your own files. We call it a reverse course: instead of you going to the course, the course comes from the document, to you, at breakfast.

This edition is one small app and two finished courses inside it. One is built from a public document every TTL has signed off on. The other is on AI and work, built from the five sources people keep misquoting in meetings. Both took an afternoon. The third course is yours, and it takes ten minutes.

1 · Take

The ESF in five mornings

The Environmental and Social Framework is 121 pages, mandatory, and the one document every TTL has certified they understand. Most of us remember three of the ten standards. We gave the public PDF to an AI with one instruction: five mornings, teach the actual content, quote the page. Then we opened the PDF at every page the AI cited and looked for the sentence. All five were there. We still checked, and so should you.

2 · Make

Now do it with yours

The same page turns any document into five mornings. The prompt is written; you add the file. It works with Copilot Cowork, with Claude Cowork and with ChatGPT, and the rule for which one is the same rule as always.

Open "Make one" and copy the prompt. Type the document's name and who the course is for ("new team members; find any covenant in under a minute").

Give the prompt and the document to your AI. Internal document (a PAD not yet disclosed, your OM, the legal agreement, anything from WBDocs): Copilot Cowork or Copilot Chat, with the file attached. Public document (a disclosed PAD, a WDR, a published ICR): Copilot, ChatGPT or Claude all work; paste the link or attach the PDF.

Paste what comes back. The AI answers with a block of text that starts with a curly bracket. Paste it into the page. It checks the shape and builds the course. Morning 1 is open.

If you live in Cowork, its scheduled prompts can send you one lesson a morning instead, and you skip the page entirely. We have not tested five mornings in a row that way; if you do, reply and tell us whether Morning 3 arrived.

To share a course with your team, download the file from the course page and send it. They load it on the same page. No accounts, no server, nothing leaves anyone's device. A course for a new team member's first week is a file someone makes once.

Check before you trust

The page references are the whole point. Open the document at the page the lesson cites and read the sentence. If it is not there, the AI invented it, and you have learned two things: one about the document and one about the AI. Five checks, five mornings, and you will know the document better than the person who wrote it. A page-number test is also the fastest way to find out whether your AI read the file or only the first twenty pages.

3 · Read

AI and work: the evidence, in five mornings

The second finished course is for the question you get at dinner, "so is AI taking the jobs?", and for the team meeting where someone quotes a LinkedIn post. Five mornings, five primary sources, every number checked against the original PDF or page, with the link in the lesson.

  • Morning 1: WDR 2026, The Promise of Artificial Intelligence. Adopt, adapt, advance; small AI; and why only 4.5 percent of jobs in low- and middle-income countries are automatable by generative AI, against 14.2 percent in rich ones.
  • Morning 2: Canaries in the Coal Mine. Brynjolfsson, Chandar and Chen on US payroll data: no economy-wide displacement, but 22 to 25 year olds in AI-exposed jobs are now 19 percent below where they should be, through hiring, not layoffs. The WDR cites the 13 percent version from 2025; the August 2026 revision says 19. A course built from the source catches that. A memory does not.
  • Morning 3: two field experiments, one jagged line. 758 BCG consultants and 776 Procter and Gamble professionals: inside the frontier one person with AI equals a small team; outside it, the same person is confidently wrong.
  • Morning 4: the Anthropic Economic Index. What millions of real AI conversations are about (a third documents, a third explanations, a sixth code), and the survey where one in ten fear for their own job and a third fear for the junior next to them.
  • Morning 5: Ethan Mollick. The four rules of Co-Intelligence, his February guide to the agentic era, and the new book, Co-Existence, out last week. Plus, for the education crowd, a sibling course of fifty studies at EvidencIA Papers.

Reality check

  • This does not replace reading the legal agreement before you sign it. It replaces not reading it afterwards.
  • The AI will sometimes cite a page that says something slightly different. That is why the page is there. Treat a wrong page as a finding, not a failure.
  • Three minutes is the design. If the AI returns 600-word lessons, you have a document again. Ask for shorter.
  • Internal documents stay inside the Bank's tools. The public chatbots are for public PDFs, and a disclosed PAD is public; the draft on your desktop is not.
  • Courses live on the device where you built them. The share button makes a file; the file is the course. Keep it in the project folder next to the thing it teaches.

Five mornings, fifteen minutes, one document fewer in the folder called Final.

Then you can say you have read it. Which is more than most of us could say last week.
