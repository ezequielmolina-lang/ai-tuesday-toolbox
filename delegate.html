Hire 1 · the pain: the background note due tomorrow

Researcher: the junior who reads everything

The CD asks for "a quick note on where the sector reform stands" before Thursday. You know the answer is spread across six aide-memoires, forty emails, two ministry announcements and a press piece you half remember. Researcher reads all of it, your work content and the web, and returns a structured report with citations.

Where it is: in Copilot, open Agents in the left panel and pick Researcher, or type @Researcher in the chat box. Under the prompt, the Sources button lets you choose work content, web, or both. It will ask you two or three clarifying questions first. Answer them. That is how you steer it. Then it works for 5 to 45 minutes depending on the task.
Delegation brief to steal (paste into Researcher)

Prepare a 2-page background note on the status of [sector] reform in [country] for a Country Director who has not followed the file. Sources: my emails, Teams chats and files about [project name] from the last 12 months, plus public news and government sites. Structure: (1) what changed since the last mission, (2) key actors and their current positions, (3) what is still blocked and why, (4) three open questions I should raise with the Vice-Minister. Flag anything where our internal documents and public statements disagree. Cite every claim. Plain language, no jargon.

Why this brief works: it names the audience, the sources, the structure and the thing to flag. Researcher is excellent at the fourth item, the contradictions between what we wrote internally and what the Ministry said publicly, which is exactly what you want to know before walking into the room.

Outside the Bank, public information only: ChatGPT Deep Research, Gemini Deep Research and Claude's research mode do the same long-running, cited job on the open web. Perfect for the country and sector landscape. Never for your internal files.
Hire 2 · the pain: the spreadsheet question at 6pm

Analyst: ask your Excel a question

Portfolio review tomorrow. "Which projects are behind on disbursement relative to time elapsed?" You know how to do it: a helper column, a formula, a pivot, twenty minutes and one mistake. Analyst does it differently: it reads the file, plans the analysis, writes and runs real Python code, checks itself, and hands you the table, the chart and the explanation. You can click to expand every step and see the code it ran. This is not a chatbot guessing an average. It is computing it.

Where it is: Agents → Analyst, or @Analyst in chat. Attach your Excel or CSV (or several: it can join them). Ask in plain English. Expand "steps" to see the Python.
Delegation brief to steal (attach your file, paste into Analyst)

Using the attached portfolio file: for each project, compute the share of time elapsed between effectiveness and closing date as of today, and the share of commitment disbursed. Rank projects by the gap (time elapsed minus disbursed), largest gap first. Show a table and a scatter chart (time elapsed vs disbursed) with project IDs as labels. Then list the projects closing within 12 months that are below 70% disbursed. Explain your method in three sentences a non-economist can follow.

Try it in two minutes, no real data needed. We made an invented portfolio (24 projects, fake IDs, fake TTLs, two sheets) so you can test Analyst before trusting it with yours. Five questions are written inside the file.

Download the sample portfolio (Excel) →

The one rule: check one row by hand. Pick a project, compute the disbursement rate yourself, compare. If it matches, trust the rest of that answer. Thirty seconds, and it is the difference between a tool and a liability in front of a Country Director.

Outside the Bank: ChatGPT and Claude both run code on an uploaded CSV in the same way. Fine for public datasets (WDI, ESCALE, the sample file above). Not for project data.
Hire 3 · the pain: re-attaching the same six files every week

Notebooks: pin your project once, ask forever

Every Copilot chat starts from zero. So every week you attach the PAD, the last ISR, two aide-memoires and the results framework again, and explain the project again. Copilot Notebooks fixes that: you create a notebook for the project, pin the reference files once (Word, PowerPoint, Excel, PDF, OneNote pages), and every prompt inside that notebook is grounded in them. References stay live: when the ISR is updated, the answers update.

Where it is: in the Copilot app, Notebooks in the left panel (also inside OneNote). Create one per project. Add references, then chat. It can also generate Word, Excel and PowerPoint outputs from the pinned content. Share the notebook with your team and they get the same grounded answers from the first prompt.
Three prompts that only make sense inside a project notebook

1. Draft the "Implementation status" section of the next ISR from the latest aide-memoire, keeping the structure of the previous ISR. Mark every number you took from a document with its source. 2. Which indicators in the results framework have no update since the last two ISRs? List them with the last reported value. 3. The new Country Manager has 10 minutes. Write the one-page brief on this project, from the pinned documents only.

A smaller set of authoritative references beats a big messy pile. Pin the five documents that matter, not the whole SharePoint.

Outside the Bank: NotebookLM is the same idea for public documents (and it now makes video overviews in 80 languages). Gemini Enterprise is piloted for staff; check what your team is allowed to load.

How to brief an agent (it's how you'd brief a good STC)

Delegation fails for the same reason it fails with humans: a vague ask. "Look into the education sector" gets you a Wikipedia summary. The briefs above share one shape. Use it for anything you delegate, to Copilot or to a person:

Goal + audience

What is this for, who will read it, what decision does it feed.

Sources

Which files, which period, work content or web or both. Name them.

Structure + length

The sections you want, in order, and how long. "2 pages" beats "brief".

What to flag

Contradictions, gaps, anything below a threshold. The thing a junior would miss.

And then the step people skip: answer the clarifying questions. Researcher asks them for a reason. Thirty seconds there saves you a wrong 40-minute report.

The reality check

It takes minutes, not seconds. Researcher can run 10 to 45 minutes on a big ask. That is the point. Start it, go to your meeting, read it after.

Citations are claims, not proof. Open two of them. Researcher is good, and it still occasionally cites an email for something the email does not quite say.

Analyst shows its work. Look at it. Expand the steps. If the Python filtered out half your rows because of a date format, you will see it there and nowhere else.

English works best. The agents handle Spanish and Portuguese, but briefs in English get more reliable results today. Ask for the output in the language you need.
