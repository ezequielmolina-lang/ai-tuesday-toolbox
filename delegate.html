First, how to brief an agent

Delegation fails for the same reason it fails with humans: a vague ask. "Look into the education sector" gets you the first paragraph of the last CPF. Brief an agent the way you would brief a good short-term consultant (STC):

Goal + audience

What is this for, who reads it, what decision it feeds.

Sources

Which files, which period, work content or web or both. Name them.

Structure + length

The sections you want, in order, and how long. "2 pages" beats "brief".

What to flag

Contradictions, gaps, anything below a threshold. The thing a junior would miss.

And the step people skip: the agent asks two or three clarifying questions before it starts. Answer them. That is how you steer it. Then go to your meeting.

Hire 1 · the pain: the background note due tomorrow

Researcher: the junior who reads everything

The STC who asks three clarifying questions before starting, which already puts them ahead of most of us.

The CD asks for "a quick note on where the sector reform stands" before Thursday. The answer is spread across six aide-memoires, forty emails, two ministry announcements and a press piece you half remember. Researcher searches all of it, including the press piece, and comes back with citations.

Where: Agents in the left panel, or @Researcher in chat. The Sources button under the prompt sets work content, web, or both; set it, the words in your brief don't. It sees Outlook, Teams, OneDrive and SharePoint. WBDocs and the Operations Portal are invisible to it, so attach the aide-memoires. It runs 10 to 40 minutes.
Brief to steal. Replace the two brackets, set Sources to work + web, stay for the questions, then go.

Prepare a 2-page background note on the status of [sector] reform in [country] for a Country Director who has not followed the file. Use my emails, Teams chats and attached files from the last 12 months, plus public news and government sites. Structure: (1) what changed since the last mission, (2) key actors and their current positions, (3) what is still blocked and why, (4) three open questions for the Vice-Minister. Flag anything where our internal documents and public statements appear to disagree, quoting both passages verbatim so I can check. Cite every claim. Plain language.

If one email is critical, name it in the brief: it can miss one. And open two citations before you trust the rest. Researcher is good, and it still occasionally cites an email for something the email does not quite say. Open every citation behind a "contradiction" before you repeat it outside the Bank.

Hire 2 · the pain: the spreadsheet question at 6pm

Analyst: ask your Excel a question

The one who shows their Python unprompted.

Portfolio review tomorrow. "Which projects are behind on disbursement relative to time elapsed?" You know how to do it: a helper column, a formula, a pivot, twenty minutes and one mistake. Analyst reads the file, writes and runs real Python, and hands you the table, the chart and the explanation. Expand "steps" and you see the code. This is not a chatbot guessing an average. It is computing it.

Where: @Analyst in chat, attach your Excel or CSV. Best run by whoever already builds the portfolio pivot; forward them this section.
Brief to steal (attach your file first)

First tell me how many projects you found and which columns you will use for effectiveness date, closing date, commitment and disbursed; stop and ask if any is ambiguous. Then, as of [today's date], compute for each project the share of time elapsed between effectiveness and revised closing date, and the share of commitment disbursed. Rank by the gap (time elapsed minus disbursed), largest first. Show a table and a scatter chart with project IDs as labels. List the projects closing within 12 months that are below 70% disbursed (70% is my illustration, not a Bank threshold). Explain your method in three sentences.

Try it in two minutes with no real data. We made an invented portfolio (24 projects, IDs FAKE-1001 to FAKE-1024, TTLs who are all dead scientists, none of them behind on anything they could be blamed for). The brief is inside the file. Start a new chat before you attach a real one.

The two checks: compute one project's rate by hand, and compare the project count with your file. One right row says nothing about rows the Python quietly dropped on a date format. Thirty seconds. It is the difference between a tool and a liability in front of a Country Director.

Hire 3 · the desk you give your hires

Notebooks: pin your project once, ask forever

Not a hire. The first shared drive that has read the shared drive.

Every Copilot chat starts from zero. It is a new STC every Monday, and you are the onboarding. Copilot Notebooks fixes that: one notebook per project, pin the PAD, the last ISR, two aide-memoires and the results framework once, and every prompt inside it can draw on them. Pin the five documents that matter, not the whole SharePoint, and pin the cleared versions, never a working draft.

Where: Notebooks in the left panel of the Copilot app. The Bank also has NotebookLM Enterprise through the Gemini Enterprise pilot (log in from the pilot page, never from notebooklm.google.com, which is public-only): stronger with long PDFs, and it makes audio and video overviews, which is more attention than the average ISR gets. Share only with licensed staff who already have access to the pinned files; never widen folder permissions to make it work.
Two prompts that only make sense inside a project notebook

1. Draft the narrative of the "Implementation status" section of the next ISR from the latest aide-memoire, keeping the structure of the previous ISR. The ISR is disclosed and the aide-memoire is not: flag every sentence you took from the aide-memoire that is not already in a disclosed document. No ratings, no risk flags. Mark every number with its source. 2. The new Country Manager has 10 minutes. Write the one-page brief on this project from the pinned documents only.

For ISRs, the sanctioned reference is still the Smart ISR Guide in the AI Tuesday resources.

Hire 4 · the pain: you are the automation

Copilot Cowork: the one that does the work, with your approval

The one who drafts the email and, unusually, asks before sending.

Researcher writes. Analyst calculates. Cowork acts. Flip the toggle at the top of the Copilot app from Chat to Cowork and describe an outcome. It plans the steps, works across Outlook, Teams, calendar and files, and asks before anything leaves your hands.

It runs on a schedule

Paste once. Activate for four Mondays, then decide.

Every Monday at 8am: check my inbox and Teams for anything urgent from the weekend; list what people are expecting from me with due dates; list what I asked colleagues for in the last two weeks and haven't received; flag which meetings this week need preparation and what. Put it in a summary in my inbox I can read in two minutes.

That is last month's My Week ritual, finally happening on a Monday.

It reacts, then acts

Describe the trigger and the job. Internal threads only.

Whenever the Legal counterpart emails me about the [project] amendment, summarize it, pull the last three exchanges on the topic, and draft a reply for my review. Check my sent emails from the last two weeks to colleagues inside worldbank.org that haven't received a reply. Skip anything to my CD or CM. Draft a polite follow-up for each, in the tone of the original thread. Do not send anything: save every draft in Drafts and list them for me.

The lines not to cross. Correspondence from government counterparts, co-financiers or the Board follows the CMU's channel: do not automate replies to it. If you have delegate access to someone else's mailbox, Cowork has it too: do not run inbox automations on a delegated mailbox without that person's agreement. It sees everything you have access to, including things you have forgotten you have access to: review what it pulls. Approve drafts one by one, and delete the schedule when you change roles.
Where: toggle Chat / Cowork at the top of m365.cloud.microsoft (not Claude's Cowork, a public-information tool). If the toggle isn't there yet, it's rolling out, which at the Bank means check again Thursday. Microsoft's documentation says it asks before every send; check that yours does on the first run.

An experiment we are sharing for comments

What if your AI answered from your sector's best studies?

Agents research the whole web, and the whole web includes vendor blogs. So a few of us in Education built a connector (an MCP server) that makes Claude or ChatGPT answer "what works in AI tutoring?" from a curated base of rigorous studies, with every number tied to the passage it came from so you can check it in one click. It is a personal pilot, not an ITS-approved tool: a few dozen core studies, three people checking every number by hand, at evidencia-mcp.vercel.app. Personal account, public questions only. Every sector has twenty studies everyone should be citing and a thousand pages nobody reads. If your GP would want one, reply and we'll share the method, and ask ai@ where such a thing should live.

A note on the day Messi retires

Don't delegate the free kick

Leo Messi retires today. In twenty-odd years he delegated plenty: the running back, most of the defending, the press conferences when he could. He never delegated the free kick. The thing only he could do, he did himself, every time, with everyone watching.

Same rule here. Delegate the reading, the counting, the first draft, the forty emails. Do not delegate the free kick: the judgment call, the conversation with the Vice-Minister, the decision on what goes in the ISR, and the last read of anything with your name on it. Researcher writes the note. You decide what it means. If an agent's report goes to the CD without you having read every line, you have not delegated. You have abdicated.
